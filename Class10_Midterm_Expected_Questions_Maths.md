## Chapter 1: Real Numbers

**Quick revision:** HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers • A decimal terminates only if the denominator (in simplest form) is of the form 2ᵐ5ⁿ • To prove irrationality, assume rational, reach contradiction.

### Section A (1 mark)
1. Express 140 as a product of its prime factors. `[Ans: 2² × 5 × 7]`
2. Given that HCF(306, 657) = 9, find LCM(306, 657). `[Ans: 22338]`
3. Find the greatest number which divides 70 and 125, leaving remainders 5 and 8 respectively. `[Ans: HCF(65,117) = 13]`
4. Is there any value of n for which 6ⁿ ends with the digit 0? Give reason. `[Ans: No]`

### Section B (2 marks)
5. Find the HCF of 96 and 404 by prime factorisation, then find their LCM using HCF × LCM = product. `[Ans: HCF = 4, LCM = 9696]`
6. Prove that 6ⁿ cannot end with the digit 0 for any natural number n.

### Section C (3 marks)
7. Prove that √5 is irrational. *(Most repeated question in this chapter)*
8. Prove that 5 − √3 is irrational.
9. Find the HCF and LCM of 12, 15 and 21 by the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic. Verify that HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers for the pair 12 and 15.

### Section D (4 marks)
10. Find the greatest number which divides 615 and 963, leaving remainder 6 in each case. `[Ans: HCF(609, 957) = 87]`
11. Find the least number which, when divided by 12, 15, 20 and 27, leaves remainder 7 in each case. `[Ans: LCM(12,15,20,27)=540, so 547]`

## Chapter 2: Polynomials

**Quick revision:** For ax² + bx + c with zeroes α, β → α + β = −b/a, αβ = c/a • Polynomial with given sum S and product P of zeroes: k[x² − Sx + P].

### Section A (1 mark)
1. If one zero of x² + 3x + k is 2, find the value of k. `[Ans: −10]`
2. If α, β are zeroes of x² − x − 6, find α + β and αβ. `[Ans: 1, −6]`
3. If the zeroes of ax² + bx + c (c ≠ 0) are equal, what can you say about the signs of a and c? `[Ans: They are the same]`
4. Find the zeroes of x² − 3. `[Ans: ±√3]`

### Section B (2 marks)
5. Find the zeroes of 6x² − 7x − 3 and verify the relationship between the zeroes and the coefficients. `[Ans: 3/2, −1/3]`
6. Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are ¼ and −1. `[Ans: 4x² + 3x − 1]`

### Section C (3 marks)
7. If α, β are the zeroes of x² − 4x + 1, find: (i) 1/α + 1/β (ii) α² + β² (iii) 1/α² + 1/β². `[Ans: 4, 14, 14]`
8. Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes have sum ½ and product −3/2. `[Ans: 2x² − x − 3]`
9. Divide x³ − 3x² + 5x − 3 by x² − 2 and find the quotient and remainder. `[Ans: quotient x − 3, remainder 7x − 9]`

### Section D (4 marks)
10. On dividing x⁴ − 6x³ + 16x² − 25x + 10 by x² − 2x + k, the remainder comes out to be x + a. Find k and a. *(NCERT classic — very important)* `[Ans: k = 5, a = −5]`
11. If α, β are zeroes of x² − 5x + 6, form a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are α + 1/β and β + 1/α. `[Ans: 6x² − 35x + 49]`

## Chapter 3: Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables

**Quick revision:** Unique solution: a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂ • Infinitely many: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂ • No solution: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂. For equations reducible to linear form, substitute 1/x = u, 1/y = v.

### Section A (1 mark)
1. Write the condition for the pair a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0 and a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0 to have a unique solution. `[Ans: a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂]`
2. How many solutions does the pair 2x + 4y = 3 and 12x + 24y = 9 have? `[Ans: Infinitely many]`
3. If x = 2, y = 3 is a solution of 2x + 3y = k, find k. `[Ans: 13]`

### Section B (2 marks)
4. Solve x + y = 5 and 2x − 3y = 4 by elimination. If y = mx + 3 is also satisfied by this solution, find m. `[Ans: x = 19/5, y = 6/5, m = −9/19]`
5. Find k for which the system 3x + y = 1 and (2k − 1)x + (k − 1)y = 2k + 1 has no solution. `[Ans: k = 2]`

### Section C (3 marks)
6. Determine a and b for which 2x + 3y = 7 and (a − b)x + (a + b)y = 3a + b − 2 has infinitely many solutions. `[Ans: a = 5, b = 1]`
7. A father says: "Seven years ago I was seven times as old as you, and three years from now I shall be three times as old as you." Find their present ages. `[Ans: Father 42, daughter 12]`
8. A fraction becomes 1 if 1 is added to the numerator and 1 subtracted from the denominator. It becomes ½ if 1 is added only to the denominator. Find the fraction. `[Ans: 3/5]`

### Section D (4 marks)
9. Solve graphically: x − y + 1 = 0 and 3x + 2y − 12 = 0. Also find the coordinates of the vertices of the triangle formed by these lines and the x-axis. `[Ans: Solution (2, 3); vertices (−1, 0), (2, 3), (4, 0)]`
10. A train covers a distance at a uniform speed. If the train had been 10 km/h faster, it would have taken 2 hours less; if 10 km/h slower, it would have taken 3 hours more. Find the distance. `[Ans: 600 km]`
11. A boat goes 30 km upstream and 44 km downstream in 10 hours; it can go 40 km upstream and 55 km downstream in 13 hours. Find the speed of the boat in still water and the speed of the stream. `[Ans: Boat 8 km/h, stream 3 km/h]`

## Chapter 4: Quadratic Equations

**Quick revision:** For ax² + bx + c = 0 → Discriminant D = b² − 4ac • Equal roots ⟺ D = 0 • Roots by factorisation (splitting the middle term) and by the quadratic formula x = [−b ± √D]/2a.

### Section A (1 mark)
1. If x = 3 is a root of kx² − 7x + 12 = 0, find k. `[Ans: 1]`
2. Find the discriminant of 2x² − 4x + 3. `[Ans: −8]`
3. Find c so that x² + 2x + c = 0 has equal roots. `[Ans: 1]`

### Section B (2 marks)
4. Solve by factorisation: x² − 3x − 10 = 0. `[Ans: 5, −2]`
5. If x = 2 is one root of 2x² + kx − 6 = 0, find k and the other root. `[Ans: k = −1, other root −3/2]`
6. Solve: √2x² + 7x + 5√2 = 0. `[Ans: −√2, −5/√2]`

### Section C (3 marks)
7. Solve 2x² − 7x + 3 = 0 by completing the square (or the quadratic formula). `[Ans: 3, ½]`
8. Find k so that kx(x − 2) + 6 = 0 has two equal roots. `[Ans: k = 6]`
9. The sum of the squares of two consecutive positive integers is 365. Find the integers. `[Ans: 13 and 14]`
10. The sum of two numbers is 15 and the sum of their reciprocals is 3/10. Find the numbers. `[Ans: 5 and 10]`

### Section D (4 marks)
11. A train travels 360 km at a uniform speed. If the speed had been 5 km/h more, it would have taken 1 hour less. Find the speed of the train. `[Ans: 40 km/h]`
12. A motorboat whose speed is 18 km/h in still water takes 1 hour more to go 24 km upstream than to return downstream. Find the speed of the stream. `[Ans: 6 km/h]`
13. Find two consecutive positive even integers whose squares add up to 340. `[Ans: 12 and 14]`

## Chapter 5: Arithmetic Progressions

**Quick revision:** aₙ = a + (n − 1)d • Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n − 1)d] • Also Sₙ = n/2 (first term + last term) • Term from the end: count backwards from the last term with difference −d.

### Section A (1 mark)
1. Find the 10th term of the AP: 2, 7, 12, … `[Ans: 47]`
2. If a = 7 and d = −3, find a₁₂. `[Ans: −26]`
3. Which term of the AP 21, 42, 63, 84, … is 420? `[Ans: 20th]`

### Section B (2 marks)
4. Which term of the AP: 3, 8, 13, 18, … is 78? `[Ans: 16th]`
5. Check whether −150 is a term of the AP: 11, 8, 5, 2, … `[Ans: No (n would not be a positive integer)]`
6. How many two-digit numbers are divisible by 3? `[Ans: 30]`

### Section C (3 marks)
7. Find the sum of the first 15 multiples of 8. `[Ans: 960]`
8. Find the sum of all two-digit numbers divisible by 7. `[Ans: 728]`
9. If the 11th term of an AP is 38 and the 16th term is 73, find the 31st term. `[Ans: 178]`
10. Find the 20th term from the end of the AP: 3, 8, 13, …, 253. `[Ans: 158]`

### Section D (4 marks)
11. If m times the mth term of an AP equals n times its nth term, prove that the (m + n)th term is 0. *(R.S. Aggarwal classic)*
12. If the ratio of the sums of n terms of two APs is (7n + 1) : (4n + 27), find the ratio of their 9th terms. `[Ans: 24 : 19]`
13. Which is the first negative term of the AP: 121, 117, 113, …? `[Ans: 32nd term]`
14. Find the sum of the first 40 positive integers divisible by 6. `[Ans: 4920]`

## Chapter 6: Coordinate Geometry

**Quick revision:** Distance = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²] • Point dividing A(x₁,y₁)B(x₂,y₂) in ratio m:n = ((mx₂+nx₁)/(m+n), (my₂+ny₁)/(m+n)) • Midpoint = average of coordinates. *(Note: if your school has dropped the section formula from the syllabus, skip Q9, Q11, Q12 part.)*

### Section A (1 mark)
1. Find the distance of the point (−6, 8) from the origin. `[Ans: 10 units]`
2. Find the distance between (2, 3) and (4, 1). `[Ans: 2√2]`
3. Find the distance of P(3, −4) from the x-axis and from the y-axis. `[Ans: 4 units; 3 units]`

### Section B (2 marks)
4. Find the point on the x-axis which is equidistant from (2, −5) and (−2, 9). `[Ans: (−7, 0)]`
5. Find y if the distance between P(2, −3) and Q(10, y) is 10 units. `[Ans: y = 3 or −9]`

### Section C (3 marks)
6. Find the coordinates of the point which divides the line segment joining (−1, 7) and (4, −3) in the ratio 2 : 3. `[Ans: (1, 3)]`
7. Find the coordinates of the points of trisection of the segment joining (4, −1) and (−2, −3). `[Ans: (2, −5/3) and (0, −7/3)]`
8. In what ratio does (−4, 6) divide the line segment joining A(−6, 10) and B(3, −8)? `[Ans: 2 : 7]`
9. If P(k − 1, 2) is equidistant from A(3, k) and B(k, 5), find k. `[Ans: k = 1 or 5]`

### Section D (4 marks)
10. If (1, 2), (4, y), (x, 6) and (3, 5) are the vertices of a parallelogram taken in order, find x and y. `[Ans: x = 6, y = 3]`
11. Find the area of the rhombus whose vertices, taken in order, are (3, 0), (4, 5), (−1, 4) and (−2, −1). *Hint: area = ½ × product of diagonals.* `[Ans: 24 sq. units]`
12. Show that the points (5, −2), (6, 4) and (7, −2) are the vertices of an isosceles triangle.

## Exam Tips (for scoring full marks)
- **Show every step** — CBSE marking is stepwise. In linear equations, write the equations, the method, the working, and box the final answer.
- **Always verify** zeroes–coefficient relationships (Ch 2) and HCF × LCM (Ch 1) when asked; they carry marks.
- In **word problems** (Ch 3 & 4), clearly state what x and y represent, form both equations, and end with a one-line conclusion in words.
- For **graphical questions** (Ch 3 & 6), use a sharp pencil, label axes, plot at least 3 points per line, and mark the intersection clearly.
- For **irrationality proofs**, write the standard contradiction structure: assume rational = p/q (coprime) → show a prime divides both p and q → contradiction.
- Attempt questions in the order: Ch 1 → Ch 6 (short, sure marks) if you feel nervous; otherwise follow the paper order. Do the 4-markers with the questions you are most confident in first.
